# PC Accelerator Preview — Need for Speed: Motor City (June 2000)

**Source:** PC Accelerator — Collector's Edition Final Issue (June 2000)  
**Preservation Date:** 2026

---

## Overview

This preview from PC Accelerator's final issue (June 2000) provides a rare snapshot of Motor City Online during its development as **Need for Speed: Motor City** — before the pivot to a purely online MMO format. The preview describes a game that still retained significant single-player elements and was targeting a 2000 release.

## Key Details from the Preview

### Title
- **Working Title:** Need For Speed: Motor City
- The preview explicitly states: "If this doesn't sound like the Need For Speed you remember, it's not. In a sense, this actually is your daddy's Need For Speed."

### Planned Release
- **Release Date:** "This year" (2000)
- The game ultimately shipped in October 2001 — more than a year later than planned

### Car Roster
- **35 car models** planned
- Range: 1932 Ford to 1973 Firebird
- The final game launched with over 60 cars

### Tracks
- **12 tracks** planned
- The final game shipped with 24 tracks

### Single-Player Modes
The preview describes two distinct single-player modes that did not survive the pivot to MMO:

**Sanctioned Mode:**
- Players start with a basic car ("the local lemon")
- Build reputation to enter restricted races
- Progress toward challenging the "neighborhood king"
- Car titles on the line in high-stakes races

**Outlaw Mode:**
- Rowdier racing against aggressive police ("the angriest smokey's this side of Salem, Oregon")
- Evade law enforcement during races

### Customization
- **Over 1,000 parts** planned (final game shipped with 2,000+)
- Parts included "everything you could imagine from blowers to fuzzy dice"
- Real-world manufacturers mentioned

### Physics
- "Completely redesigned true-point physics engine"
- Modifications expected to make "noticeable difference, right down to staggered tires"

### Multiplayer
- Described as a "massive multiplayer experience"
- Racing for "pink slips" (car titles)
- The preview treats multiplayer as a feature rather than the core experience

### Producer
- **Producer:** Jim Eomunos
- This may be a typo or an early producer who left the project; later interviews and articles reference **Michael Waite** as producer

### Audio
- Sound crew "hard at work ensuring that the audio remains true to your individual"
- (Text cuts off here in the OCR)

## Evolution from Preview to Final Product

| Feature | June 2000 Plan | October 2001 Final |
|---------|---------------|-------------------|
| Title | Need for Speed: Motor City | Motor City Online |
| Release | 2000 | October 31, 2001 |
| Focus | Single-player + multiplayer | Online MMO only |
| Cars | 35 | 60+ |
| Tracks | 12 | 24 |
| Parts | 1,000+ | 2,000+ |
| Single-player modes | Sanctioned, Outlaw | None (EZ Street tutorial only) |
| Producer | Jim Eomunos | Michael Waite |

## Preservation Context

This preview is significant because:

1. It documents the game before the MMO pivot — when it still had substantial single-player content
2. It shows the ambitious scope that was planned for a 2000 release
3. The single-player modes (Sanctioned and Outlaw) were entirely removed from the final product
4. It provides a baseline for understanding how much the game changed during development

PC Accelerator itself is a notable source — the magazine's "final issue" status makes this one of the last print previews of the game before its transformation into an online-only title.
