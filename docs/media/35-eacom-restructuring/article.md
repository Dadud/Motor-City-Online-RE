# EA.com Restructuring and MCO Discontinuation

**Source:** Electronic Arts Inc. SEC Filings (Form 10-K FY2003, Form 10-Qs 2002–2003)  
**Date:** Fiscal 2002–2003  
**Preservation Date:** 2026

---

## Overview

Motor City Online was a casualty of Electronic Arts' broader restructuring of its EA.com business segment — a division created to house the company's online gaming initiatives. The restructuring, announced in October 2001 (just weeks after MCO's launch), led to the discontinuation of several subscription-based online offerings and ultimately contributed to the game's shutdown in August 2003.

## The EA.com Business Segment

EA.com was established as a separate business unit within Electronic Arts to develop and operate subscription-based online games. The segment included:

- **Motor City Online** (launched October 29, 2001)
- **Earth & Beyond** (launched September 2002)
- **Pogo.com** (casual games portal)
- **Kesmai** (acquired online game service)

## October 2001 Restructuring Announcement

According to EA's SEC filings, in October 2001 — the same month Motor City Online launched — EA announced a restructuring plan for the EA.com business segment. The plan involved "strategic decisions to discontinue" certain offerings that were not meeting projected cash flow expectations.

From the Form 10-Q for the quarter ended June 30, 2002:

> "Accordingly, we did not realize our projected cash flows and discontinued these offerings as part of EA.com's restructuring plan in fiscal 2002."

## MCO's Place in the Restructuring

While the SEC filings group Motor City Online with other discontinued offerings, the specific financial impact is documented in the impairment charges recorded by EA.com:

- **Fiscal 2002:** EA.com recorded restructuring charges related to the discontinuation of underperforming subscription services
- **Fiscal 2003:** Additional impairments were recorded for Pogo and Kesmai finite-lived intangible assets as the restructuring continued
- By December 31, 2002, **$905,000 remained** in the EA.com accrued restructuring balance

## The Bigger Picture: EA.com Losses

The SEC filings reveal that EA.com was consistently unprofitable during MCO's lifetime:

| Fiscal Period | EA.com Performance |
|---------------|-------------------|
| FY2002 | Significant losses; restructuring initiated |
| Q1 FY2003 (Jun 2002) | Continued losses expected for full year |
| FY2003 | Full-year losses; Earth & Beyond also discontinued |

## Connection to MCO's Shutdown

While EA never explicitly stated "the restructuring killed Motor City Online," the timeline is revealing:

1. **October 29, 2001:** MCO launches
2. **October 2001:** EA announces EA.com restructuring
3. **Fiscal 2002:** MCO included in discontinued offerings
4. **August 29, 2003:** MCO servers shut down
5. **March 2003:** EA's FY2003 10-K notes the continued EA.com losses

The restructuring suggests that MCO was under financial pressure from the very beginning of its commercial life — a difficult position for any new online service.

## Preservation Context

The SEC filings provide the only official, public documentation of EA's rationale for scaling back its online gaming ambitions during this period. Unlike the game's launch — which was accompanied by press releases and media coverage — the discontinuation was handled quietly through financial disclosures.

This documentation helps explain why a game that earned generally favorable reviews (7.6–7.9/10 from major outlets) was nonetheless shuttered after less than two years: it was part of a broader corporate decision to abandon the subscription-based PC online gaming model.
