# IGN Review: Motor City Online

**Source:** IGN  
**Date:** November 16, 2001  
**Reviewer:** Dan Adams  
**Score:** 7.9/10  
**Archived:** <https://www.ign.com/articles/2001/11/17/motor-city-online>

---

## Overview

IGN's Dan Adams reviewed Motor City Online shortly after its October 2001 launch, awarding it a 7.9/10. While praising the core concept, Adams raised concerns about player population, technical issues, and whether the subscription model was justified.

## Key Excerpts

### The Concept
> *"The idea behind Motor City Online is a darn good one. It just makes you get all flustered and hot under the collar about racing cars... I mean, c'mon, the idea of a massively multiplayer racing game is pretty freaking fantastic. The thoughts of Cannonball Run and Driver race through my head when I hear that, but it just isn't the case."*

### Player Population
> *"Not only is it not really massively multiplayer, but at times it can be hard to tell that it's multiplayer at all. The lack of players in the game to compete against is a huge drawback from what would probably have been a fun, if flawed, experience."*

> *"Unfortunately, when you go into the races, there are very few games available even at peak hours of the day. There were times when I sat with a game created waiting for someone to come along and join for a long, long time."*

### Community Features
> *"It's basically a great community site for people to get together, race, and trade cars and parts in a slightly RPGesque leveling up way... The community tools are pretty good, if a little slow, and trading cars, racing for pink slips, running auctions, and checking out time trial bests is all good."*

> *"It does a commendable job at bringing gamers into and making them feel a part of the community as a whole."*

### RPG Progression
> *"While the game is mostly about racing, it also has an RPG sort of structure to it. When you start off in the game, you'll be considered a newbie. After garnering enough experience points, which you collect by completing races and getting good times in time trials, you'll increase in level and eventually in rank. Every time you increase in level, you'll get a pay increase."*

### Economics
> *"There's a dynamic economics system in the game that will actually react to supply and demand and as prices fluctuate, enterprising individuals will have the opportunity to make some bank and get a pretty good garage full of cars."*

> *"It's pretty hard to start out because you have very little money... you'll need to race in a lot of sponsored races to get the money to even come close to being competitive with the rest of the racers with your own jitney."*

### Part Customization
> *"Actually buying parts and upgrading your car can be a little bit of a pain. The interface in this particular section, when it should be easier, is a little slow, clunky and really doesn't give enough information that a layperson like myself can understand."*

> *"As it is, I pretty much just guessed and went with some of the higher priced parts because they must be better, right?"*

### Graphics
> *"Even running the thing on a Athlon Thunderbird 1.4 with 512 MB RAM and a GeForce3, it's not that pretty. And when running on a PIII 600 with 256MB of RAM and a GeForce2, there was a little slowdown. Not so good."*

### Latency and Bugs
> *"There's plenty of problems with lag where you'll see someone just go speeding by you at what looks like incredible speeds, and can therefore cause a whole lot of problems when it comes to accidents."*

> *"There's bugs, network problems, and I lost track of how many times the game crashed so completely that I had to hit the power button on my computer."*

### Collision Physics
> *"I've found that it's pretty easy to totally ruin one of your opponent's chances by barely nudging them in the back corner of the car. More often than not it will be enough to send them spinning off into a wall, sometimes taking out a couple other cars in the process."*

> *"There's nothing more frustrating than being poked in the rear by a dirty driver although it's hard to complain because accidents really do happen and when there is no real penalty for causing them, no one is going to be inclined to be really careful."*

### Subscription Value
> *"So the question you really have to ask yourself before you go out and plunk a bunch of money on the game is it really worth the extra $10 a month to play this? I don't think so. There's just not enough going on here. This isn't a massively multiplayer persistent world we're talking about here. It's community tools. And as of right now there doesn't seem to be much of a community."*

> *"If more people had the game and were willing to pay the price of membership, there would be more value here... But as it is, only racing game freaks are really going to want to shell the monthly fee out for this one and there aren't nearly as many of those as there are RPG or FPS freaks."*

### Final Assessment
> *"Even though this is a good idea for a community and the bugs and crashes might even be semi-tolerable if there were more people playing, I can't really recommend that people buy this one unless they are part of that real racing freak section of the gaming populace. Of course the paradox there is if they are racing freaks, then they might not like the average racing model presented to them."*

---

## Preservation Context

Dan Adams's review captures a critical tension in MCO's commercial story: the game was conceptually appealing but struggled to justify its $10/month subscription in a market where racing game enthusiasts were far outnumbered by RPG and FPS players.

Several of Adams's observations proved prophetic:

- **Player population issues:** The "lack of players" he noted at launch would become a chronic problem that ultimately contributed to the shutdown
- **Subscription skepticism:** His doubt that the game justified its monthly fee foreshadowed the commercial struggles that would lead EA to pull the plug in 2003
- **Technical instability:** Crashes requiring hard reboots and severe lag were cited by multiple reviewers as significant barriers

The review's closing paradox — that "racing freaks" were the target audience but might not appreciate the "average racing model" — highlights the design challenge MCO faced in trying to balance accessibility with depth.
