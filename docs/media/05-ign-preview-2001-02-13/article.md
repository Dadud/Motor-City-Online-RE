**Source:** IGN PC

## Key Details

- **Original Name:** Need For Speed: Motor City
- **Release Window:** Summer/Fall 2001 (pushed back from Fall 2000)
- **Developer:** EA Seattle
- **Publisher:** Electronic Arts
- **Platform:** Windows PC

## Evolution from NFS: Motor City to Motor City Online

The development team made a conscious change from a hardcore racing game to a community-focused online experience, taking cues from MMORPGs like EverQuest and Ultima Online. Players can still swap pink slips, lose money in big races, and win pro race prizes, but now also:
- Join clubs
- Attend swap meets
- Read up-to-date news about Motor City
- Track who's winning big and whose car is turning heads

## Game Modes

### Sim World
The primary mode. Players can:
- Sell, trade, and customize cars
- Enter official or unofficial races
- Win prize money in pro races
- Wager cars in pink-slip races
- Bet cold hard cash

### Arcade Action
A jump-in race mode without financial risk:
- No money, cars, or inventory at stake
- Still gain points toward level progression
- Points accumulate at a lower rate than Sim World
- Designed as an on-ramp for new online racers

## Cars & Customization

- **~50 licensed cars at launch** from Ford, Chevrolet, and Chrysler
- Additional cars planned as post-launch downloads
- **Eight dealerships** to buy from, seven of which are auctions
- Real supply/demand and auction models drive prices

### Parts & Upgrades
- Licensed parts from **Edelbrock, Holley, and Hurst**
- **1,000+ different parts** available
- Cosmetic tweaks: custom hubs, license plates, paint jobs, fuzzy dice
- Performance upgrades: bigger engine blocks, blowers, spoilers

## Club System

Similar to clans in Quake:
- Join like-minded racers for training, protection, and parts
- Own turf in parts of the city
- Defend territory from rival clubs
- Cash rewards for winning races and recruiting new players
- Specialization opportunities:
  - Mechanics build perfect cars for team racers
  - Buyers outbid competition for best parts

## Engine & Physics

- New **four-point physics engine** built specifically for the game
- Fully rendered cockpits
- Environment effects (e.g., tornado during farm race)
- 13 different neighborhoods/track types:
  - Drag racing
  - Circuit tracks
  - Open/outlaw street races

## E3 2000 Update (from same article)

- First deeper look after debut at E3 1999
- Confirmed shift toward sustainable online universe
- Economy driven by players, not pre-set values
- Virtual town paper for news: cop locations, hangouts, parts sources
- NPC mayor calls shots for the city
- EA staffers staffed as in-game competitors/chatters
- 30 licensed cars from 1930s–1970s at that time
- Plans for real hot-rod owners to judge virtual car shows with cash prizes
- Sponsored events with real industry racers

## 1999 First Look (from same article)

- Originally announced as Need For Speed: Motor City
- 40+ licensed classics: 1932 Ford Coupe, 1957 Chevy Bel-Air, 1963 Corvette Stingray, 1966 Pontiac GTO, 1970 Mustang Boss 302
- Two racing types: street-racing challenge (quarter-mile) and sanctioned road courses (13 tracks)
- Deep customization: engine, muffler, rims, paint, graphics, ground kits, chop tops
- Online "community" features: hangouts like Sal's Drive-In and Roxie's Diner
- Money system: start with set cash, buy fixer-uppers, race for dollars or pink slips
